Physics Model, Assumptions, and Limitations

This is a numerical educational model. It is intended to help investigate qualitative effects of initial position, damping, mass, and magnetic strength. It does not claim to be a complete or experimentally accurate model of every real magnetic pendulum.

Governing Equations

m d²x/dt² = Fₓ − c dx/dt
m d²y/dt² = Fᵧ − c dy/dt

rᵢ = √[(x − Xᵢ)² + (y − Yᵢ)² + h²]
Fₓ = Σ A(Xᵢ − x)/rᵢ³
Fᵧ = Σ A(Yᵢ − y)/rᵢ³

Chosen Geometry and Numerical Settings

  • Three magnets form a triangle at approximately (−0.065, −0.040), (0.065, −0.040), and (0.000, 0.070) m.
  • The vertical separation is h = 0.018 m. This prevents the force from becoming singular when the pendulum is horizontally near a magnet.
  • The integration time step is Δt = 0.002 s, using fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4). Smaller time steps can be tested for numerical stability.
  • A trajectory is treated as settled after its speed stays below a small threshold for a specified time. A maximum time is also used.

Important Assumptions

  • The pendulum moves as a point particle in a horizontal two-dimensional plane.
  • Gravity is balanced by the support and does not directly accelerate horizontal motion.
  • The damping force is linear: −cv.
  • The pendulum begins from rest in this draft.
  • No external force or energy input is included after release.

Limitations and Questions to Test

  • The magnetic-force formula is a simplified approximation, not an exact electromagnetic interaction model.
  • With damping, the displayed total mechanical energy should generally decrease. Small numerical fluctuations may occur because this is a discrete calculation.
  • Very low damping may prevent settling during the displayed simulation time.
  • Whether tiny changes in initial position lead to different final magnets depends on the selected parameters and should be tested, not assumed.
